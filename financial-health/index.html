What drove it

  • Water bills catching up. No water bills posted in July or August, so several arrived and were paid together in September.
  • Three months of landscaping. Landscape service invoices for three months were billed at once.
  • One-time repairs. Spa repairs, pool gate fob repairs, concrete work at the pool, and a call box microphone replacement.
  • Annual election and office costs. Election administration and supplies, which land early in the fiscal year.

Spread over July through September, operating spending was within about 2% of budget. The Board will keep a close eye on water, which remains above plan even after the timing evens out.

Treasurer's note for November

The Association closed its 2025–26 fiscal year on August 31 in good financial shape. Day-to-day operating costs finished about 7% under budget, revenue came in above plan thanks largely to interest earned on our reserve investments, and the year ended with an operating surplus.

Our reserve fund, which pays for long-term projects such as gates, fencing, paving, and the pool, grew about 17% during the year. That growth came after paying for this year's gate repairs and other projects directly from reserves rather than through a special assessment. Every scheduled contribution was made, and the new 2026–27 budget increases the monthly reserve contribution by about 15% to keep building toward the reserve study's recommended level.

September, the first month of the new fiscal year, showed higher-than-usual spending. That is mostly timing: water and landscaping bills that had not posted over the summer arrived together, along with several one-time pool and gate repairs and our annual election costs. Looked at across July through September, spending was essentially on budget, and operating cash remains comfortably above the Board's minimum. As always, the complete monthly financial statements are available to every member through our management company.

What the Board is watching

  • Water costs. Above budget last year and still running above plan; the new budget allows more for water, and usage is under review.
  • Pool and spa costs. Finished last year about 27% over budget; the service contract is being monitored closely.
  • Reserve investments. The CD that matured this summer has been reinvested, all deposits are within FDIC insurance limits, and another CD matures in early November.
  • Reserve study update. The 2026–27 study is underway and will refresh our long-term funding targets.
