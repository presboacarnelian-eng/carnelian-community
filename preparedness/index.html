Emergency Preparedness & Disaster Recovery

A practical Carnelian guide to preparing your household, receiving local alerts, learning emergency skills and knowing where to turn after an earthquake, storm, power outage or other major emergency.

Start Here

Four things every household can do now

Preparedness is most useful when it is simple, practiced and shared with everyone in the household.

1

Know your risks

For Gardena and the South Bay, prepare for earthquakes, severe weather and flooding, utility outages, fires and other local emergencies.

2

Make a family plan

Choose meeting places, an out-of-area contact, evacuation routes and a plan for children, older adults, disabilities and pets.

3

Build your supplies

Keep water, nonperishable food, medications, lighting, batteries, first aid, cash, copies of key documents and a portable go-bag.

4

Get alerts & training

Register for local alerts, enable phone emergency warnings and consider CERT, CPR or first-aid training before an emergency happens.

Build Your Kit

Plan for at least the first few days

Ready.gov recommends a basic supply kit that includes at least a three-day supply of water and nonperishable food, plus supplies matched to your household’s needs.

💧

Food & water

  • Water for drinking and sanitation
  • Nonperishable food
  • Manual can opener
  • Pet food and special dietary items
🔦

Light, power & information

  • Flashlights and spare batteries
  • Battery or hand-crank radio
  • Phone power banks and charging cables
  • Local maps and important phone numbers
🩹

Health & personal needs

  • First-aid supplies and medications
  • Eyeglasses and mobility needs
  • Hygiene and sanitation supplies
  • Copies of IDs, insurance and key records
Tip: Keep a smaller go-bag ready for evacuation and consider a separate kit for your vehicle. Review supplies periodically for expired food, water, medications and batteries.

California’s Earthquake Warning system can provide valuable seconds of notice before strong shaking reaches your location.

📳

Earthquake Warning California →

🪑

Drop, Cover & Hold On

If you receive a warning or feel shaking: drop to the ground, take cover under sturdy furniture when available, and hold on until the shaking stops.

🏠

Reduce hazards at home

Secure tall furniture and heavy objects, know where utility shutoffs are located, keep shoes and a flashlight accessible, and have a qualified professional properly secure your water heater.

During & Immediately After

Protect people first

Follow official emergency instructions and do not enter damaged or unsafe areas just to inspect property.

🚨

Life safety

  • Call 911 for immediate threats to life or safety.
  • Check yourself and household members for injuries.
  • Expect aftershocks after an earthquake.
  • Stay away from downed power lines and visibly damaged structures.
🔥

Natural gas safety

SoCalGas advises residents not to shut off the gas meter unless they smell gas, hear escaping gas or see another sign of a leak—and only when it is safe. If the meter is shut off, do not turn it back on yourself.

SoCalGas emergency guidance →

🤝

Check on neighbors safely

Once your household is safe, check on nearby neighbors who may need help, especially older adults or people with access and functional needs. Do not place yourself in danger or enter damaged structures.

Recovery

What to do after the immediate danger

Recovery can take time. Document conditions, protect your household from secondary hazards and use verified public resources.

1

Document damage

When it is safe, take photos or video of damage before cleanup and keep receipts for emergency repairs and temporary expenses.

2

Contact insurance

Report covered damage promptly and follow your insurer’s instructions before authorizing major repairs whenever possible.

3

Use verified assistance

If a disaster is federally declared, check FEMA and DisasterAssistance.gov for available programs. Be cautious of unsolicited contractors or requests for payment.

4

Report common-area issues

After life-safety concerns are addressed, report Carnelian common-area damage, access problems or other Association concerns to property management.
